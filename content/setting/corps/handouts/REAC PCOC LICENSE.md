### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
REAC respectfully requests a fifteen-year license extension to continue providing reconnaissance, engineering, and general fixer services to extraction corps and frontier outposts in the Periphery zone. REAC has maintained continuous service across seventeen active systems, responded to 1,247 distress calls in the past licensing period, and achieved a mission completion rate of 71.9%, a figure that, while below industry standard, exceeds most expectations given our resource constraints.

#### 2.5 Notes to Financial Statements
**Note 1: Going Concern** 
The auditor has, in four consecutive engagements, declined to issue an unqualified going concern opinion, citing REAC's debt load relative to liquid assets, its dependency on informal creditor forbearance (see Note 2), and "a business model that appears to run substantially on momentum." REAC's official response, entered into the record at Theodosia Kessler's insistence, is that the charter has never missed a payroll in sixty-eight years and does not intend to start now. The balance sheet in 2.2 was reconstructed from four separate crew ledgers.

**Note 2: Related Party Transactions** 
C. Malachi Industries provides repair services to REAC at "cost." The definition of "cost" has never been formally established. Malachi's invoices frequently include line items such as "emotional labor," "witnessing," and "the sheer entertainment value of your disaster logs." REAC has never contested these charges.

**Note 3: Contingent Liabilities (as of FY8048 close)**
- Waste disposal fine: fully accrued on the balance sheet above (see 2.2); appeal outcome pending.
- No other contingent liabilities were outstanding as of the FY8048 close. See Note 4 for matters arising after year-end.

**Note 4: Subsequent Events** 
The following matters arose after the FY8048 balance sheet date (8048.12.30) but before this filing (8049.3.14), and are disclosed here rather than reflected in the FY8048 statements above:
- PRAC arbitration claim, 22,000cr (potential), filed 8049, arising from the Node 5 extraction-fee dispute.
- License renewal itself, filed 8049.3.14, 24 days late; see 4.1.
- Creditor balances for C. Malachi Industries and Pavel Medical, LLC were paid down during this period; see updated figures in 1.3.

**Note 5: Fleet Reconstitution (8047)** 
In 8047, an explosion at Halyard Reach (Node 4) destroyed the majority of Crew A and Crew B's active fleet. The bulk of both divisions' current vessels were acquired as secondhand refits in the period following the incident, financed substantially through the Hazemeyer Credit AG term loan referenced in 1.3 and 2.2. The origin of the fleet-wide collateral arrangement, rather than any single prior acquisition. Kael Kessler assumed the newly created Finance Controller position that same year to manage the resulting debt structure and creditor relationships. This incident is also the origin of Division C: Vinny Kessler's proposal to establish a presence at Farside (Node 6) was raised and approved during the same period, while family attention and resources were primarily directed toward rebuilding Divisions A and B.

#### 4.4 Ethics & Conduct

REAC maintains an informal code of conduct:
1. **Show up.** If you said you'd be there, be there. Even if it's dangerous. Especially if it's dangerous. That's the job.
2. **Finish the job.** "Mostly" counts. "Eventually" counts. "We tried" does not count unless everyone died trying.
3. **Don't steal from crew.** Stealing from clients is negotiable. Stealing from corps is expected. Stealing from crew is unforgivable.
4. **Leave no one behind.** Unless they're already dead. Or unless going back gets more people killed.
5. **Report anomalies.** Not to reporters. To PCOC in writing after encounters.
