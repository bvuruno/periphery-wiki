# anomalies
**nobody calls them "aliens"**. that word implies a species, a homeworld, something with an intent you could negotiate with. the working term is *anomalies*. there is no shared origin, no single species, no motive. whatever gets filed under "*anomaly*" is united by one thing: it doesn't make sense, and it usually doesn't end well for whoever finds it. it is common knowledge they exist, but their nature and reason remain unknown.
## classification
classification is done **by behaviour**, from least to most dangerous:
### t class - tech
**touches signal, data, memory, comms**. a relay station running faster than physics should allow. nobody wants to ask why, because the answer might mean turning it off. the most commercially exploited class, and the most quietly dangerous, because someone is always tempted to keep using it rather than file a report.
### s class - structural/spatial
doesn't act, **exists wrong.** a room bigger inside than out, doors that open onto the wrong side of the ship, a section of space where time moves slightly slower than it should. no hostility, no comprehensible mechanism, no way to "fight" it. usually handled by containment rather than resolution.
### p class - predator/predatory
**hunts humans specifically or incidentally.** the xenomorph end of the spectrum. uncommon, and those who encounter them rarely survive to report. which is why these are the most mythologised and least understood. most of what's "known" about class p anomalies is secondhand, embellished, or both.
### c class - cognitive
the most dangerous to categorise, because it **acts on minds** rather than bodies or machines. compulsions, false memories, groups that develop identical dreams. nobody agrees where this category ends and mundane madness begins. reports are unreliable by definition, since the reporter's own mind is the compromised instrument.

## field procedure
encounters with anomalies must be reported to the **[[the periphery/2 the corps/2.0 regulatory bodies/PCOC\|PCOC]]'s** ***anomaly incident division*** **(AID)** through a written report.
the procedure for encountering an *anomaly* is as follows:
1. do not engage
2. confirm it's actually reportable
3. take note of time, coordinates, behaviour
4. identify class
5. if you're not sure what happened, file it as unknown
